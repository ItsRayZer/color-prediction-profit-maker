How the edge search works

An "edge" means a pattern that wins often enough, often enough times, that it is very unlikely to be luck. The system is built to be hard to fool, including by itself.

1

A collector gathers every result

Every 2 minutes, a small program checks the live WinGo results feed for all four games (30s, 1m, 3m, 5m) and stores every new ball: its number, colour, and size, plus the full result record. Nothing is picked by hand and nothing is skipped.

2

Each round is described by 44 features

For every round, the system works out 44 facts about what came before it: current streaks, how often results alternate, whether the game is in a streaky or choppy mood, what the other games are doing, and more. These facts are what signals look at when deciding whether to fire.

3

50 fixed signals are tested, the same list every time

The candidate signals were written down in advance and never change: base patterns, regime signals that react to streaky or choppy stretches, signals using the full result record, and cross-game signals. Fixing the list in advance stops the search from inventing a new "pattern" to fit whatever luck just happened.

4

Testing is walk-forward: signals are graded on rounds they never saw

For each game, the first half of its history is context (the background a signal is allowed to look at) and the second half is the test. A signal only earns credit for rounds in the test half. This is the difference between "this pattern fits the past" and "this pattern actually predicted what came next."

5

A signal must clear all three gates on the test data

Enough test rounds, a win rate that stays above the bar even after a strict safety margin, and a check that it beats pure chance once you account for testing 50 signals at once. Miss any one gate and it does not count. The gates are spelled out below.

6

The verdict is binary, and you get pinged only on a real find

If a signal clears all three gates, the result is EDGE_FOUND and you get a message. Otherwise the result is NO_EDGE, nothing changes, and the search quietly keeps collecting. No "almost an edge" alerts, no hype.

The three gates

All three must pass on the held-out test rounds, for one game at a time. One gate failing is enough to reject the signal.

GATE 1

Enough evidence

The signal must have fired on at least 100 test rounds. A pattern seen a handful of times proves nothing, however good it looks.

≥ 100test rounds minimum
GATE 2

A win rate above the bar, with a safety margin

The signal's win rate is shrunk by a strict statistical margin (the Wilson lower bound). Even after that shrink, it must still beat 53.02%, roughly the break-even line for these bets.

≥ 53.02%Wilson lower-bound win rate
GATE 3

Beats chance, honestly

Testing 50 signals means some will look good by pure luck. This check corrects for that: the odds of the result being a fluke must be under 5%.

Honest notes

The "extra" data turned out to be mostly echoes

The full result record was checked field by field. The premium field is just the ball number repeated, sum is always 0, and the feed's own colour label matches the colour already worked out from the number. No hidden signal there, but everything is stored anyway, so nothing is lost if that ever changes.

A separate backtest found the same thing

Before the collector existed, 140 prediction models from the app were tested against 80 rounds of real history, graded only on rounds they had not seen. Zero cleared the bar. The best raw scores were small-sample noise, the same trap the gates are built to reject.

The bar does not move

The 53.02% requirement, the 100-round minimum, and the 5% fluke limit stay exactly where they are. They change only on the owner's explicit word, never because a result looks tempting.

No profit is promised, ever

WinGo results may simply be random, in which case the correct long-term answer is NO_EDGE forever, and this system will keep saying so. Its job is to tell the truth about the data, not to manufacture a reason to bet.
